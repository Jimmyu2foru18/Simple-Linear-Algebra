Orthogonality & Projections

Comprehensive review of orthogonal vectors and subspaces, projections onto subspaces, least squares approximation, orthogonal matrices, and the Gram-Schmidt process. Covers chapters 14–17.

1. Orthogonal Vectors & Subspaces

Two vectors $\mathbf{x}$ and $\mathbf{y}$ are orthogonal if their dot product is zero: $\mathbf{x}^T\mathbf{y} = 0$. Two subspaces $S$ and $T$ are orthogonal if every vector in $S$ is orthogonal to every vector in $T$.

1.1 Dot Product

$$\mathbf{x}^T\mathbf{y} = x_1y_1 + x_2y_2 + \cdots + x_ny_n$$
Dot product of vectors $\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^n$

1.2 Orthogonal Complements

The orthogonal complement of a subspace $S \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is:

$$S^\perp = \{\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^n : \mathbf{x}^T\mathbf{y} = 0 \text{ for all } \mathbf{y} \in S\}$$
Orthogonal complement

1.3 Fundamental Theorem

Orthogonality of the Four Fundamental Subspaces:
  • Row space is orthogonal to nullspace ($C(A^T) \perp N(A)$)
  • Column space is orthogonal to left nullspace ($C(A) \perp N(A^T)$)
  • Together, each pair spans the whole space: $C(A^T) \oplus N(A) = \mathbb{R}^n$

2. Projections onto Subspaces

The projection of a vector $\mathbf{b}$ onto a subspace $S$ is the vector $\mathbf{p} \in S$ that is closest to $\mathbf{b}$. The error $\mathbf{e} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{p}$ is orthogonal to $S$.

2.1 Projection Formula

If $S$ has an orthonormal basis $\{\mathbf{q}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{q}_r\}$, then:

$$\text{proj}_S\mathbf{b} = (\mathbf{q}_1^T\mathbf{b})\mathbf{q}_1 + \cdots + (\mathbf{q}_r^T\mathbf{b})\mathbf{q}_r = Q(Q^T\mathbf{b})$$
Projection onto subspace $S$

2.2 Projection Matrix

$$P = Q(Q^TQ)^{-1}Q^T = QQ^T \quad \text{(when $Q$ has orthonormal columns)}$$
Projection matrix onto column space of $Q$

Properties: $P^2 = P$ (idempotent) and $P^T = P$ (symmetric).

3. Least Squares Approximation

When $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ has no solution, the least squares solution minimizes $\|A\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{b}\|^2$. This occurs when the error vector $\mathbf{e} = A\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{b}$ is orthogonal to the column space of $A$.

3.1 Normal Equations

$$A^TA\mathbf{x} = A^T\mathbf{b}$$
Normal equations for least squares

3.2 Geometric Interpretation

The least squares solution $\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ produces the projection of $\mathbf{b}$ onto $C(A)$. The residual $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{b} - A\hat{\mathbf{x}}$ is orthogonal to $C(A)$.

Important: $A^TA$ is invertible if and only if $A$ has linearly independent columns. This is the key condition for a unique least squares solution.

4. Gram-Schmidt Orthogonalization

The Gram-Schmidt process transforms a linearly independent set $\{\mathbf{a}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{a}_n\}$ into an orthogonal set $\{\mathbf{q}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{q}_n\}$ spanning the same subspace.

4.1 The Process

$$\mathbf{q}_1 = \mathbf{a}_1$$ $$\mathbf{q}_2 = \mathbf{a}_2 - \frac{\mathbf{q}_1^T\mathbf{a}_2}{\mathbf{q}_1^T\mathbf{q}_1}\mathbf{q}_1$$ $$\mathbf{q}_3 = \mathbf{a}_3 - \frac{\mathbf{q}_1^T\mathbf{a}_3}{\mathbf{q}_1^T\mathbf{q}_1}\mathbf{q}_1 - \frac{\mathbf{q}_2^T\mathbf{a}_3}{\mathbf{q}_2^T\mathbf{q}_2}\mathbf{q}_2$$
Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization steps

4.2 QR Factorization

$$A = QR$$
QR factorization: $Q$ orthogonal, $R$ upper triangular

The Gram-Schmidt process produces the QR factorization, which is useful for solving least squares problems and eigenvalue algorithms.
