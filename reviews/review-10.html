Positive Definite & SVD

Covers positive definite matrices and minima, similar matrices and Jordan form, and singular value decomposition. Review of chapters 28, 29, and 30.

1. Positive Definite Matrices

A symmetric matrix $A$ is positive definite when $x^T A x > 0$ for all nonzero $x$. This connects multivariable optimization to linear algebra through the Hessian matrix.

1.1 Tests for Positive Definiteness

TestCondition
Eigenvalue testAll eigenvalues $\lambda_i > 0$
Pivot testAll pivots in Gaussian elimination are positive
Determinant testAll leading principal minors $\det(A_k) > 0$
Quadratic form$x^T A x > 0$ for all nonzero $x$
Factorization$A = LDL^T$ with positive diagonal in $D$

1.2 Completing the Square = Elimination

Key Insight: For $Q(x) = x^T A x$, completing the square reveals the pivots outside the squares and multipliers inside. Positive pivots guarantee a sum of positive squares.

For $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 6 \\ 6 & 20 \end{bmatrix}$, completing the square gives: $$Q(x) = 2(x_1 + 3x_2)^2 + 2x_2^2$$ Pivot 1 = 2, multiplier = 3, pivot 2 = 2. All positive $\Rightarrow$ positive definite.

1.3 Principal Axis Theorem

$$A = Q\Lambda Q^T$$
Spectral decomposition of positive definite matrix

The eigenvectors $q_i$ give the principal axes of the ellipsoid $x^T A x = 1$, and the eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ determine the semi-axis lengths $1/\sqrt{\lambda_i}$.

2. Similar Matrices & Jordan Form

Two matrices are similar if they represent the same linear transformation in different bases. Similarity preserves eigenvalues but changes eigenvectors. When a matrix lacks a full set of eigenvectors, the Jordan form captures the structure.

2.1 Similarity Transformation

$$B = M^{-1}AM$$
Similarity transformation
  • Similar matrices have the same eigenvalues
  • Eigenvectors transform as $x_B = M^{-1}x_A$
  • Diagonalization: $S^{-1}AS = \Lambda$ when $A$ has $n$ independent eigenvectors
  • The diagonal matrix $\Lambda$ is the "best" member of the similarity family

2.2 Jordan Blocks

$$J_k(\lambda) = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda & 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda & 1 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & \lambda & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & \lambda \end{bmatrix}$$
Jordan block of size $k$

A Jordan block $J_k(\lambda)$ has size $k$ but only one independent eigenvector. The number of Jordan blocks equals the number of independent eigenvectors. Jordan's theorem states every square matrix is similar to a block diagonal matrix of Jordan blocks.

2.3 Diagonalizable vs Non-Diagonalizable

MatrixEigenvaluesEigenvectorsDiagonalizable?
$\begin{bmatrix} 4 & 0 \\ 0 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$4, 42Yes ($= 4I$, "loner")
$\begin{bmatrix} 4 & 1 \\ 0 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$4, 41No (Jordan block $J_2(4)$)

3. Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)

The SVD is the most powerful matrix factorization: every real matrix $A$ can be written as $A = U\Sigma V^T$, where $U$ and $V$ are orthogonal and $\Sigma$ is diagonal with non-negative singular values.

3.1 The SVD Factorization

$$A = U\Sigma V^T$$
Singular Value Decomposition
  • Singular values: $\sigma_i = \sqrt{\lambda_i(A^T A)}$, ordered $\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r > 0$
  • $V$ columns are eigenvectors of $A^T A$
  • $U$ columns are found from $u_i = \frac{1}{\sigma_i} A v_i$
  • Number of nonzero singular values = rank of $A$

3.2 Outer Product Form

$$A = \sum_{i=1}^r \sigma_i u_i v_i^T$$
SVD as sum of rank-1 matrices

3.3 Four Fundamental Subspaces

SubspaceBasis from SVD
Row space $\mathcal{C}(A^T)$First $r$ columns of $V$
Null space $\mathcal{N}(A)$Last $n-r$ columns of $V$
Column space $\mathcal{C}(A)$First $r$ columns of $U$
Left null space $\mathcal{N}(A^T)$Last $m-r$ columns of $U$

3.4 Key Properties

Eckart-Young-Mirsky Theorem: The truncated SVD $A_r = \sum_{i=1}^r \sigma_i u_i v_i^T$ is the best rank-$r$ approximation to $A$ in the Frobenius norm.
