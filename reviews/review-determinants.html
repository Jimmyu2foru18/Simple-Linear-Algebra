Determinants & Cramer's Rule

Comprehensive review of determinant properties, determinant formulas and cofactors, Cramer's Rule, and geometric interpretations of determinants. Covers chapters 18–20.

1. Properties of Determinants

The determinant is a scalar function $\det: M_{n \times n}(\mathbb{R}) \to \mathbb{R}$ that measures the "volume scaling factor" of the linear transformation represented by the matrix.

1.1 Key Properties

PropertyMathematical Statement
Multiplicative$\det(AB) = \det(A)\det(B)$
Transpose$\det(A^T) = \det(A)$
Row swapSwapping two rows multiplies $\det$ by $-1$
Scalar multiple$\det(cA) = c^n\det(A)$ for $n \times n$ matrix
Triangular$\det(T) = \prod_{i=1}^n t_{ii}$ for triangular matrix $T$
Singular$A$ is invertible iff $\det(A) \neq 0$

1.2 Effect of Row Operations

  • Type I (swap): $\det$ changes sign
  • Type II (scale): $\det$ is multiplied by the scalar
  • Type III (add multiple): $\det$ unchanged

2. Determinant Formulas & Cofactors

The determinant of an $n \times n$ matrix can be computed using cofactor expansion along any row or column.

2.1 Cofactor Expansion

$$\det(A) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}C_{ij} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_{ij}C_{ij}$$
Cofactor expansion along row $i$ or column $j$

2.2 Cofactor Definition

$$C_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j}M_{ij}$$
Cofactor formula

where $M_{ij}$ is the $(i,j)$-minor: the determinant of the matrix obtained by deleting row $i$ and column $j$.

2.3 2×2 and 3×3 Formulas

$$\det\begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix} = ad - bc$$
2×2 determinant
$$\det\begin{bmatrix} a & b & c \\ d & e & f \\ g & h & i \end{bmatrix} = a(ei - fh) - b(di - fg) + c(dh - eg)$$
3×3 determinant (Sarrus' rule)

3. Cramer's Rule

Cramer's Rule provides an explicit formula for the solution of $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ when $A$ is invertible ($n \times n$).

3.1 The Formula

$$x_i = \frac{\det(A_i)}{\det(A)}$$
Cramer's Rule

where $A_i$ is the matrix obtained by replacing column $i$ of $A$ with $\mathbf{b}$.

3.2 Practical Considerations

Computational Cost: Cramer's Rule requires computing $n+1$ determinants of size $n \times n$. For large systems, this is far less efficient than Gaussian elimination or LU factorization.

4. Geometric Interpretation

The determinant has important geometric meanings in $\mathbb{R}^2$ and $\mathbb{R}^3$.

4.1 Volume Interpretation

  • 2D: $|\det(A)|$ = area of parallelogram formed by columns of $A$
  • 3D: $|\det(A)|$ = volume of parallelepiped formed by columns of $A$
  • nD: $|\det(A)|$ = n-dimensional volume of the parallelotope

4.2 Sign of Determinant

  • Positive: Orientation preserving (right-handed)
  • Negative: Orientation reversing (left-handed)
  • Zero: Transformation collapses space to lower dimension
Key Insight: The determinant tells us how much a linear transformation scales volumes and whether it preserves or reverses orientation.
