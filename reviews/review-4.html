The Four Subspaces & Applications

Comprehensive review of the four fundamental subspaces, matrix spaces and rank, and graphs, networks & incidence matrices. Covers chapters 10–12 from Gilbert Strang's MIT 18.06 course.

1. The Four Fundamental Subspaces

For an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ with rank $r$, the four fundamental subspaces completely describe the action of $A$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ and $\mathbb{R}^m$.

1.1 The Four Subspaces

SubspaceSymbolAmbient SpaceDimensionDefinition
Column Space$C(A)$$\mathbb{R}^m$$r$Span of columns of $A$
Nullspace$N(A)$$\mathbb{R}^n$$n - r$Solutions to $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$
Row Space$C(A^T)$$\mathbb{R}^n$$r$Span of rows of $A$
Left Nullspace$N(A^T)$$\mathbb{R}^m$$m - r$Solutions to $A^T\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0}$

1.2 Rank Equality

Rank Equality: The row rank equals the column rank. Both equal the number of pivots in the reduced row echelon form (RREF). This equality unifies the row space and column space dimensions.

1.3 Orthogonal Complements

The column space and nullspace of $A^T$ are orthogonal complements in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Similarly, $C(A)$ and $N(A^T)$ are orthogonal complements in $\mathbb{R}^m$.

$$C(A) \perp N(A^T) \quad \text{in } \mathbb{R}^m, \qquad C(A^T) \perp N(A) \quad \text{in } \mathbb{R}^n$$
Orthogonal complement pairs

1.4 Basis from RREF

  • Column space basis: Pivot columns of $A$ (not RREF)
  • Row space basis: Nonzero rows of RREF
  • Nullspace basis: Special solutions from $R\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$
  • Left nullspace basis: Special solutions to $A^T\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0}$

2. Matrix Spaces & Rank

Vector space concepts extend naturally to matrices themselves. The set of all $m \times n$ matrices forms a vector space, and important subspaces arise from symmetry constraints.

2.1 Matrix Vector Spaces

SpaceDescriptionDimension
$M_{m \times n}$All $m \times n$ matrices$mn$
Sym($n$)Symmetric $n \times n$ matrices$\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$
Tri($n$)Triangular $n \times n$ matrices$\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$
Diag($n$)Diagonal $n \times n$ matrices$n$
Skew($n$)Skew-symmetric $n \times n$ matrices$\frac{n(n-1)}{2}$

2.2 Rank-1 Matrices

A matrix has rank 1 if every column is a multiple of a single vector. Such matrices can be written as the outer product of two vectors.

$$A = \mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T \quad \text{(rank-1 factorization)}$$
Rank-1 matrix as outer product
Key Insight: Every matrix of rank $r$ can be expressed as a sum of $r$ rank-1 matrices. Rank-1 matrices are the "building blocks" of all matrices.

2.3 Dimension of Rank-1 Space

The dimension of the space of rank-1 matrices in $M_{m \times n}$ is $m + n - 1$: $m$ parameters for $\mathbf{u}$, $n$ for $\mathbf{v}$, minus 1 for the scaling ambiguity $c\mathbf{u} \cdot \frac{1}{c}\mathbf{v}^T = \mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^T$.

$$\dim(\{\text{rank-1 matrices in } M_{m \times n}\}) = m + n - 1$$
Dimension of rank-1 matrix space

3. Graphs, Networks & Incidence Matrices

Linear algebra provides powerful tools for analyzing networks. Incidence matrices encode graph structure, and their nullspaces reveal fundamental properties of electrical and mechanical networks.

3.1 Incidence Matrices

For a graph with $n$ nodes and $m$ edges, the incidence matrix $A$ is $m \times n$. Row $i$ (for edge $i \to j$) has $+1$ at column $i$ and $-1$ at column $j$, with zeros elsewhere.

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} +1 & -1 & 0 & \cdots \\ 0 & +1 & -1 & \cdots \\ \vdots & & & \ddots \end{bmatrix}$$
Incidence matrix structure

3.2 Rank of Incidence Matrix

Rank Theorem for Incidence Matrices: For a connected graph with $n$ nodes, $\text{rank}(A) = n - 1$. One row is always dependent because the sum of all rows is zero.

3.3 Kirchhoff's Laws

  • KCL (Kirchhoff's Current Law): $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$, current balance at each node
  • KVL (Kirchhoff's Voltage Law): $A^T\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0}$, voltage balance around loops

The dimension of $N(A^T)$ equals the number of independent cycles: $m - n + 1$.

3.4 Spanning Trees

A spanning tree of a connected graph with $n$ nodes has exactly $n-1$ edges and connects all nodes without cycles. The columns of $A$ corresponding to a spanning tree are linearly independent.

$$\text{Number of independent cycles} = m - n + 1$$
Cycle rank formula
