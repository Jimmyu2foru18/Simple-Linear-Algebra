Pseudoinverse & Final Review

Covers left and right inverses, the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse, and a comprehensive final review of all linear algebra topics. Review of chapters 34 and 35.

1. Left/Right Inverses & Pseudoinverse

When a matrix is not invertible, we can still define one-sided inverses or the full pseudoinverse. The pseudoinverse $A^+$ inverts $A$ on the row space and column space and is zero on the null spaces.

1.1 One-Sided Inverses

ConditionInverse TypeFormulaProduct Result
Full column rank ($m \geq n$, $\text{rank}=n$)Left inverse$(A^T A)^{-1} A^T$$A(A^T A)^{-1}A^T = P_{\text{col}}$
Full row rank ($m \leq n$, $\text{rank}=m$)Right inverse$A^T(AA^T)^{-1}$$A^T(AA^T)^{-1}A = P_{\text{row}}$

1.2 The Pseudoinverse via SVD

$$A^+ = V\Sigma^+U^T, \quad \Sigma^+ = \begin{bmatrix} D^{-1} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
Pseudoinverse from SVD: invert nonzero singular values, leave zeros
  • $AA^+$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal{C}(A)$
  • $A^+A$ is the orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal{R}(A)$
  • $A$ is invertible from row space to column space; $A^+$ is its inverse there
  • Minimum-norm least-squares solution: $x = A^+b$

1.3 Four Subspaces Dimensions

For $A$ $m \times n$ of rank $r$: Column space has dimension $r$, null space has dimension $n-r$, row space has dimension $r$, left null space has dimension $m-r$.

2. Final Course Review

Weaving together eigenvalues, diagonalization, projections, Markov chains, and least squares into a coherent picture of linear algebra.

2.1 Markov Chains and Steady State

$$u^{(k)} = A^k u^{(0)} \to c_1 x_1 \text{ as } k \to \infty$$
Markov chain convergence: only $\lambda = 1$ term survives
  • $\lambda = 1$ is always an eigenvalue of a Markov matrix
  • All other eigenvalues satisfy $|\lambda| < 1$
  • Steady state is the eigenvector for $\lambda = 1$, scaled by conservation of total mass

2.2 Projection onto a Subspace

$$P = A(A^T A)^{-1} A^T$$
Projection onto column space of $A$

For projection onto a line through $a$: $P = \frac{aa^T}{a^T a}$. The projected vector $Pb$ is the closest point in $\mathcal{C}(A)$ to $b$.

2.3 Least Squares

$$A^T A \hat{x} = A^T b$$
Normal equations for least-squares solution
  • $\hat{x}$ minimizes $\|Ax - b\|_2$
  • Geometrically: $A\hat{x}$ is the orthogonal projection of $b$ onto $\mathcal{C}(A)$
  • If $A^T b = 0$, the best approximation is the zero vector
  • Straight-line fit: $y = c + mt$, where $c$ is intercept and $m$ is slope

2.4 Matrices That Cannot Be $B^T B$

Quick Test: Any matrix $B^T B$ is symmetric and positive semi-definite. If $A \neq A^T$, no $B$ exists with $A = B^T B$.
