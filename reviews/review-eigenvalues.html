Eigenvalues & Diagonalization

Comprehensive review of eigenvalues, eigenvectors, diagonalization, matrix powers, differential equations, and Markov chains. Covers chapters 21–24.

1. Eigenvalues & Eigenvectors

An eigenvector $\mathbf{x}$ of a matrix $A$ is a nonzero vector such that $A\mathbf{x} = \lambda\mathbf{x}$ for some scalar $\lambda$, called the eigenvalue.

1.1 The Eigenvalue Equation

$$A\mathbf{x} = \lambda\mathbf{x} \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad (A - \lambda I)\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$$
Eigenvalue equation

1.2 Characteristic Equation

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$$
Characteristic equation

Solving $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$ gives the eigenvalues. The characteristic polynomial is $\det(A - \lambda I)$.

1.3 Key Properties

  • An $n \times n$ matrix has $n$ eigenvalues (counting algebraic multiplicity)
  • Eigenvalues of $A^T$ are the same as eigenvalues of $A$
  • Trace of $A$ = sum of eigenvalues
  • Determinant of $A$ = product of eigenvalues

2. Diagonalization

A matrix $A$ is diagonalizable if there exists an invertible matrix $S$ and a diagonal matrix $\Lambda$ such that $A = S\Lambda S^{-1}$.

2.1 Diagonalization Theorem

Diagonalization Criterion: $A$ is diagonalizable if and only if $A$ has $n$ linearly independent eigenvectors. In this case, $S = [\mathbf{x}_1 \; \mathbf{x}_2 \; \cdots \; \mathbf{x}_n]$ and $\Lambda = \text{diag}(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \ldots, \lambda_n)$.

2.2 Matrix Powers

$$A^k = S\Lambda^k S^{-1} = S\begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1^k & & \\ & \ddots & \\ & & \lambda_n^k \end{bmatrix}S^{-1}$$
Matrix powers via diagonalization

This formula allows efficient computation of high powers of $A$.

3. Differential Equations & exp(At)

The matrix exponential $e^{At}$ solves systems of linear differential equations $\frac{d\mathbf{u}}{dt} = A\mathbf{u}$.

3.1 Matrix Exponential

$$e^{At} = I + At + \frac{(At)^2}{2!} + \frac{(At)^3}{3!} + \cdots$$
Matrix exponential series

3.2 Solution Formula

$$\mathbf{u}(t) = e^{At}\mathbf{u}(0) = S\begin{bmatrix} e^{\lambda_1 t} & & \\ & \ddots & \\ & & e^{\lambda_n t} \end{bmatrix}S^{-1}\mathbf{u}(0)$$
Solution to $\frac{d\mathbf{u}}{dt} = A\mathbf{u}$

3.3 Stability

  • If all $\text{Re}(\lambda_i) < 0$: solution decays to zero (stable)
  • If any $\text{Re}(\lambda_i) > 0$: solution grows unbounded (unstable)
  • If $\text{Re}(\lambda_i) = 0$: solution oscillates or is constant

4. Markov Chains & Fourier Series

A Markov chain is a stochastic process where the next state depends only on the current state. The transition matrix $P$ has columns that sum to 1.

4.1 Markov Matrix Properties

  • All entries are nonnegative: $p_{ij} \geq 0$
  • Columns sum to 1: $\sum_i p_{ij} = 1$
  • Eigenvalue $\lambda = 1$ always exists (corresponding to steady state)

4.2 Steady State

$$P\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x}$$
Steady state equation

The steady-state vector $\mathbf{x}$ is the eigenvector corresponding to $\lambda = 1$, normalized so its entries sum to 1.

4.3 Fourier Series Connection

Fourier series decompose periodic functions into sums of sines and cosines. The Fourier matrix $F$ has entries $F_{jk} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\omega^{jk}$ where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/n}$. The FFT computes the discrete Fourier transform in $O(n\log n)$ operations.
