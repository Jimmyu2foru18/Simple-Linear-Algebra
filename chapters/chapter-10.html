Detailed Topic Breakdowns

The Four Subspaces

For an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ of rank $r$, the four fundamental subspaces are:

  • Column Space $C(A)$: In $\mathbb{R}^m$, dimension $r$. All combinations of columns of $A$.
  • Nullspace $N(A)$: In $\mathbb{R}^n$, dimension $n-r$. All solutions to $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$.
  • Row Space $C(A^T)$: In $\mathbb{R}^n$, dimension $r$. All combinations of rows of $A$.
  • Left Nullspace $N(A^T)$: In $\mathbb{R}^m$, dimension $m-r$. All solutions to $A^T\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0}$.

Orthogonal Complements

The row space $C(A^T)$ and nullspace $N(A)$ are orthogonal complements in $\mathbb{R}^n$. Similarly, $C(A)$ and $N(A^T)$ are orthogonal complements in $\mathbb{R}^m$. Every vector in one space is orthogonal to every vector in the other.

Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra

The theorem connects the four subspaces through dimensions and orthogonality. Part 1 states $C(A)$ and $N(A^T)$ are orthogonal complements in $\mathbb{R}^m$. Part 2 states $C(A^T)$ and $N(A)$ are orthogonal complements in $\mathbb{R}^n$.

Rank Equality

The row rank equals the column rank. Both equal the number of pivots in the RREF. This fundamental equality unifies the row space and column space dimensions.

Computation Examples

Derivation: Orthogonality of $C(A)$ and $N(A^T)$

If $\mathbf{y} \in N(A^T)$, then $A^T\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0}$. For any $\mathbf{b} \in C(A)$, there exists $\mathbf{x}$ with $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$. Then:

$$\mathbf{y}^T\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{y}^T(A\mathbf{x}) = (A^T\mathbf{y})^T\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}^T\mathbf{x} = 0$$

Thus $\mathbf{y} \perp \mathbf{b}$. Every vector in $N(A^T)$ is orthogonal to every vector in $C(A)$.

Derivation: Dimension Formula Verification

For an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ with rank $r$:

$$\dim(C(A)) = r$$ $$\dim(N(A)) = n - r$$ $$\dim(C(A^T)) = r$$ $$\dim(N(A^T)) = m - r$$ $$\text{Sum of dimensions in } \mathbb{R}^m: \quad r + (m-r) = m \checkmark$$ $$\text{Sum of dimensions in } \mathbb{R}^n: \quad r + (n-r) = n \checkmark$$

The orthogonal complement dimensions sum to the ambient space dimension, as expected.

Learning Strategies

Mnemonic Devices

Mnemonic: "Row and Column, Left and Right"

Remember the pairs: $C(A)$ (columns) pairs with $N(A^T)$ (left nullspace). $C(A^T)$ (rows) pairs with $N(A)$ (nullspace). Each pair lives in complementary dimensions.

Mnemonic: "Same Rank, Orthogonal Complements"

$C(A)$ and $C(A^T)$ have the same dimension (the rank). Their orthogonal complements ($N(A^T)$ and $N(A)$) fill the remaining dimensions.

Study Tips

  • Draw the "Fundamental Theorem" diagram showing the four subspaces
  • Always determine the rank first, it gives dimensions of all four subspaces
  • The row space basis comes from the nonzero rows of RREF
  • The left nullspace basis comes from the special solutions to $A^T\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0}$
  • Practice identifying which subspace a given vector belongs to

How RREF reveals bases for all subspaces:

$$N(A): \text{ solve } R\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0} \rightarrow \text{span}\left\{\begin{bmatrix}-2\\1\\0\\0\end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix}-3\\0\\-1\\1\end{bmatrix}\right\}$$ $$N(A^T): \text{ solve } A^T\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{0} \rightarrow \text{span}\left\{\begin{bmatrix}-2\\1\end{bmatrix}\right\}$$

Worked Examples

Example 1: Identifying All Four Subspaces

Problem: Find bases and dimensions for all four subspaces of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution:

  • RREF: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$, rank = 1
  • $C(A)$: basis = $\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}\}$, dim = 1
  • $N(A)$: $x_1 + 2x_2 + 3x_3 = 0$, free: $x_2, x_3$
  • Special solutions: $\begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$, $\begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$, dim = 2
  • Row space: basis = $\{[1, 2, 3]\}$, dim = 1
  • Left nullspace $N(A^T)$: $y_1 + 2y_2 = 0$, basis = $\{\begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\}$, dim = 1

Example 2: Orthogonality Verification

Problem: Verify that every vector in $C(A^T)$ is orthogonal to every vector in $N(A)$ for $A$ from Example 1.

Solution:

$$\text{Row space vector: } \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\text{Nullspace vector: } \begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\text{Dot product: } 1(-2) + 2(1) + 3(0) = -2 + 2 + 0 = 0 \checkmark$$ $$\text{Row space vector: } \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\text{Nullspace vector: } \begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$\text{Dot product: } 1(-3) + 2(0) + 3(1) = -3 + 0 + 3 = 0 \checkmark$$

Practice Problems

Problem Set 10

Problem 1: For $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$, find all four subspaces and their dimensions.

Hint: $A$ is already in RREF form.

Solution

$C(A)$: $\text{span}\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\}$, dim = 2

$N(A)$: $\text{span}\{\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\}$, dim = 1

Row space: $\text{span}\{[1, 0, 0], [0, 1, 0]\}$, dim = 2

$N(A^T)$: $\text{span}\{\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\}$, dim = 1

Problem 2: True or false: The row space of $A$ equals the column space of $A^T$. Explain.

Hint: What are the columns of $A^T$?

Solution

True. The columns of $A^T$ are the rows of $A$. The row space of $A$ is defined as the span of its rows, which equals $C(A^T)$.

Problem 3: If $A$ is $4 \times 6$ with rank 3, what are the dimensions of all four subspaces?

Hint: Apply the dimension formulas with $m=4, n=6, r=3$.

Solution

$C(A)$: dim = 3 (in $\mathbb{R}^4$)

$N(A)$: dim = $6-3 = 3$ (in $\mathbb{R}^6$)

Row space: dim = 3 (in $\mathbb{R}^6$)

$N(A^T)$: dim = $4-3 = 1$ (in $\mathbb{R}^4$)

Problem 4: Show that the left nullspace of $A$ is the orthogonal complement of $C(A)$ in $\mathbb{R}^m$.

Hint: Use the definition of $N(A^T)$ and the argument from the derivation section.

Solution

If $\mathbf{y} \in N(A^T)$, then $A^T\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{0}$. For any $\mathbf{b} \in C(A)$, $\mathbf{b} = A\mathbf{x}$. Then $\mathbf{y}^T\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{y}^T A\mathbf{x} = (A^T\mathbf{y})^T\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$. So $\mathbf{y}$ is orthogonal to all vectors in $C(A)$. Thus $N(A^T) = C(A)^\perp$.
