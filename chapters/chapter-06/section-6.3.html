Section 6.3: Orthogonal Projections

Assigned problems: 7, 10, 11, 14, 42, 43. Source: linearAlgebraSolutionsComplete.pdf (PDF Chapter 6, Section 6.3).

What is this section about?

What is an orthogonal projection? The orthogonal projection of y onto a subspace W is the closest point in W to y. If {u₁, …, u₀} is an orthogonal basis for W, then proj₇₇ y = ∑ (y·u₀)/(u₀·u₀) u₀.

Concept Overview

If $\{\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_p\}$ is an orthogonal set of vectors (i.e., $\mathbf{u}_i \cdot \mathbf{u}_j = 0$ for $i \neq j$), then it is linearly independent.

The orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{y}$ onto $W = \text{Span}\{\mathbf{u}_1, \dots, \mathbf{u}_p\}$ is:

$$\text{proj}_W \mathbf{y} = \sum_{i=1}^{p} \frac{\mathbf{y} \cdot \mathbf{u}_i}{\mathbf{u}_i \cdot \mathbf{u}_i} \mathbf{u}_i$$

Orthogonal Decomposition Theorem: Any vector $\mathbf{y}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ can be written uniquely as $\mathbf{y} = \hat{\mathbf{y}} + \mathbf{z}$ where $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ is in $W$ and $\mathbf{z}$ is in $W^\perp$ (the orthogonal complement).

Best Approximation Theorem: The orthogonal projection $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ is the closest point in $W$ to $\mathbf{y}$, meaning $\|\mathbf{y} - \hat{\mathbf{y}}\| < \|\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{v}\|$ for all $\mathbf{v}$ in $W$ with $\mathbf{v} \neq \hat{\mathbf{y}}$.

Problem 7

Problem: Given an orthogonal set $\{\mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2\}$, write $\mathbf{y}$ as the sum of a vector in $W = \text{Span}\{\mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2\}$ and a vector in $W^\perp$.

Solution

Since $\{\mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2\}$ is an orthogonal set, by the Orthogonal Decomposition Theorem:

$$\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \frac{\mathbf{y} \cdot \mathbf{u}_1}{\mathbf{u}_1 \cdot \mathbf{u}_1}\mathbf{u}_1 + \frac{\mathbf{y} \cdot \mathbf{u}_2}{\mathbf{u}_2 \cdot \mathbf{u}_2}\mathbf{u}_2$$

For the given vectors, $\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 2/3 \\ 7/3 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{z} = \begin{bmatrix} 10/3 \\ -1/2 \\ -7/3 \end{bmatrix}$, so $\mathbf{y} = \hat{\mathbf{y}} + \mathbf{z}$ where $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ is in $W$ and $\mathbf{z}$ is in $W^\perp$.

Problem 10

Problem: Given an orthogonal set $\{\mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2, \mathbf{u}_3\}$, write $\mathbf{y}$ as the sum of a vector in $W$ and a vector in $W^\perp$.

Solution

Since $\{\mathbf{u}_1, \mathbf{u}_2, \mathbf{u}_3\}$ is an orthogonal set, by the Orthogonal Decomposition Theorem:

$$\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \sum_{i=1}^{3} \frac{\mathbf{y} \cdot \mathbf{u}_i}{\mathbf{u}_i \cdot \mathbf{u}_i}\mathbf{u}_i$$

For the given vectors, $\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3/2 \\ 5/2 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{z} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ -3 \end{bmatrix}$, so $\mathbf{y} = \hat{\mathbf{y}} + \mathbf{z}$ where $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ is in $W$ and $\mathbf{z}$ is in $W^\perp$.

Problem 11

Problem: Find $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$, the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{y}$ onto $W = \text{Span}\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2\}$ where $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2\}$ is orthogonal.

Solution

By the Best Approximation Theorem, $\hat{\mathbf{y}}$ is the closest point in $W$ to $\mathbf{y}$.

$$\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \frac{\mathbf{y} \cdot \mathbf{v}_1}{\mathbf{v}_1 \cdot \mathbf{v}_1}\mathbf{v}_1 + \frac{\mathbf{y} \cdot \mathbf{v}_2}{\mathbf{v}_2 \cdot \mathbf{v}_2}\mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$$

$\hat{\mathbf{y}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$.

Problem 14

Problem: Find $\hat{\mathbf{z}}$, the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{z}$ onto $W = \text{Span}\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2\}$ where $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2\}$ is orthogonal.

Solution

By the Best Approximation Theorem, $\hat{\mathbf{z}}$ is the closest point in $W$ to $\mathbf{z}$.

$$\hat{\mathbf{z}} = \frac{\mathbf{z} \cdot \mathbf{v}_1}{\mathbf{v}_1 \cdot \mathbf{v}_1}\mathbf{v}_1 + \frac{\mathbf{z} \cdot \mathbf{v}_2}{\mathbf{v}_2 \cdot \mathbf{v}_2}\mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1/2 \\ -1/2 \end{bmatrix}$$

$\hat{\mathbf{z}} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1/2 \\ -1/2 \end{bmatrix}$.
