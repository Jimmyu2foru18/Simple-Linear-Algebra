What is this section about?

What is a coordinate system? The change-of-coordinates matrix converts coordinate vectors from one basis to another, allowing the same vector to be represented in different coordinate systems.

Core Lecture, Gilbert Strang (MIT)

Concept Overview

Let $\mathcal{B} = \{\mathbf{b}_1, \dots, \mathbf{b}_n\}$ be a basis for a vector space $V$. For any $\mathbf{x}$ in $V$, the coordinate vector of $\mathbf{x}$ relative to $\mathcal{B}$ is:

$$[\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}} = \begin{bmatrix} c_1 \\ c_2 \\ \vdots \\ c_n \end{bmatrix}$$

where $\mathbf{x} = c_1\mathbf{b}_1 + c_2\mathbf{b}_2 + \cdots + c_n\mathbf{b}_n$.

The change-of-coordinates matrix from $\mathcal{B}$ to the standard basis is:

$$P_{\mathcal{B}} = [\mathbf{b}_1 \, \mathbf{b}_2 \, \dots \, \mathbf{b}_n]$$

To convert from $\mathcal{B}$-coordinates to standard coordinates: $\mathbf{x} = P_{\mathcal{B}}[\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}}$.

To convert from standard coordinates to $\mathcal{B}$-coordinates: $[\mathbf{x}]_{\mathcal{B}} = P_{\mathcal{B}}^{-1}\mathbf{x}$.

Worked Problems from linearAlgebraSolutionsComplete.pdf

Problem 1

Problem: We calculate $x = c_1\mathbf{b}_1 + c_2\mathbf{b}_2$ where $\mathbf{b}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{b}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} -5 \\ -6 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

$$x = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & -5 \\ 4 & -6 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} c_1 \\ c_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ -6 \end{bmatrix}$$

$x = \begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ -6 \end{bmatrix}$.

Problem 2

Problem: We calculate $x = c_1\mathbf{b}_1 + c_2\mathbf{b}_2$ where $\mathbf{b}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{b}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 8 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

$$x = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 8 \\ 2 & 5 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} c_1 \\ c_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -4(5) \\ 5(7) \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix}$$

$x = \begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix}$.

Problem 4

Problem: We calculate $x = c_1\mathbf{b}_1 + c_2\mathbf{b}_2 + c_3\mathbf{b}_3$ where $\mathbf{b}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{b}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 0 \\ 9 \end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{b}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

$$x = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 4 & 1 \\ 3 & 0 & 1 \\ 2 & 9 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} c_1 \\ c_2 \\ c_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -7 \\ 5 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

$x = \begin{bmatrix} -7 \\ 5 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$.
