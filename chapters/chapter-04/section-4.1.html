Section 4.1: Vector Spaces and Subspaces

Assigned problems: 1, 2, 4, 5. Source: linearAlgebraSolutionsComplete.pdf (PDF Chapter 4, Section 4.1).

What is this section about?

What is a subspace? A subspace is a subset of a vector space that contains the zero vector and is closed under vector addition and scalar multiplication.

Concept Overview

A subspace of a vector space $V$ is a subset $H$ of $V$ that satisfies three properties:

  • The zero vector of $V$ is in $H$.
  • For each $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}$ in $H$, the sum $\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}$ is in $H$ (closed under addition).
  • For each $\mathbf{u}$ in $H$ and scalar $c$, the vector $c\mathbf{u}$ is in $H$ (closed under scalar multiplication).

By Theorem 1, if $H = \text{Span}\{\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_p\}$, then $H$ is a subspace of $V$.

Worked Problems from linearAlgebraSolutionsComplete.pdf

Problem 1

Problem: Determine whether the given set $V$ is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$. If $u$ and $v$ are in $V$, then their entries are nonnegative.

Solution

a. If $u$ and $v$ are in $V$, then their entries are nonnegative. Since a sum of nonnegative numbers is nonnegative, the vector $u + v$ has nonnegative entries. Thus $u + v$ is in $V$.

b. Example: If $\mathbf{u} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$ and $c = -1$, then $\mathbf{u}$ is in $V$ but $c\mathbf{u}$ is not in $V$. Since $V$ is not closed under scalar multiplication, $V$ is not a subspace.

Problem 2

Problem: Determine whether the given set $W$ is a subspace.

Solution

a. If $\mathbf{u} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \end{bmatrix}$ is in $W$, then the vector $c\mathbf{u} = \begin{bmatrix} cx \\ cy \end{bmatrix}$ is in $W$ because $2(cx)(cy) = c^2(2xy) \geq 0$ since $xy \geq 0$.

b. Example: If $\mathbf{u} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -7 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$, then $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are in $W$ but $\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}$ is not in $W$. $W$ is not closed under addition.

Problem 4

Problem: Example showing that $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are on a line $L$, but $\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}$ is not.

Solution

Note that $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$ are on the line $L$, but $\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}$ is not. Since $L$ is not closed under addition, $L$ is not a subspace.

Problem 5

Problem: Determine whether the given set is a subspace.

Solution

Yes. Since the set is $\text{Span}\{\mathbf{v}\}$, the set is a subspace by Theorem 1.

The set is a subspace.
