Section 3.5: The Dimension of a Vector Space

Assigned problems: 1, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 19, 21, 29. Source: linearAlgebraSolutionsComplete.pdf (PDF Chapter 4, Section 4.5).

What is this section about?

What is dimension? The dimension of a vector space is the number of vectors in any basis for the space. The Rank Theorem states that rank A + dim Nul A = n.

Core Lecture, Gilbert Strang (MIT)

Concept Overview

If a vector space $V$ has a basis $\mathcal{B} = \{\mathbf{b}_1, \dots, \mathbf{b}_n\}$, then any set in $V$ containing more than $n$ vectors must be linearly dependent.

The dimension of a nonzero vector space $V$ is the number of vectors in a basis for $V$.

Rank Theorem: For an $m \times n$ matrix $A$, $\text{rank } A + \dim(\text{Nul } A) = n$.

Worked Problems from linearAlgebraSolutionsComplete.pdf

Problem 1

Problem: Find the dimension of $H = \text{Span}\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2\}$ where $\mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

Since $\mathbf{v}_1$ and $\mathbf{v}_2$ are not multiples of each other, $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2\}$ is linearly independent and is thus a basis for $H$. Hence the dimension of $H$ is 2.

Problem 7

Problem: Find the dimension of $H = \text{Nul } A$ where $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

Row reducing $A$ gives the identity matrix, so the homogeneous system has only the trivial solution. Thus $H = \text{Nul } A = \{\mathbf{0}\}$, and the dimension of $H$ is 0.

Problem 9

Problem: Find the dimension of $H = \left\{\begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \\ a \\ b \end{bmatrix} : a, b \text{ in } \mathbb{R}\right\}$.

Solution

This subspace is $\text{Span}\left\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\right\}$. Since these vectors are not multiples of each other, the dimension of $H$ is 2.

Problem 11

Problem: Find the dimension of $\text{Col } A$ where $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 9 \\ 0 & 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

The matrix $A$ with these vectors as columns row reduces to show two pivot columns. Thus the dimension of $\text{Col } A$ is 2.

Problem 13

Problem: Find the dimension of $\text{Col } A$ and $\text{Nul } A$ where $A$ is in echelon form with three pivot columns and two columns without pivots.

Solution

There are three pivot columns, so $\dim(\text{Col } A) = 3$. There are two columns without pivots, so the equation $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$ has two free variables. Thus $\dim(\text{Nul } A) = 2$.

Problem 15

Problem: Find the dimension of $\text{Col } A$ and $\text{Nul } A$ where $A$ is in echelon form with two pivot columns and two columns without pivots.

Solution

There are two pivot columns, so $\dim(\text{Col } A) = 2$. There are two columns without pivots, so the equation $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$ has two free variables. Thus $\dim(\text{Nul } A) = 2$.

Problem 19

Problem: True/False: The plane must pass through the origin to be a subspace.

Solution

False. The plane must pass through the origin; see Example 4.

Problem 21

Problem: Show that the Hermite polynomials form a basis for $\mathbb{P}_3$.

Solution

The matrix whose columns are the coordinate vectors of the Hermite polynomials has 4 pivots, so its columns are linearly independent. Since their coordinate vectors form a linearly independent set, the Hermite polynomials themselves are linearly independent in $\mathbb{P}_3$. Since there are four Hermite polynomials and $\dim \mathbb{P}_3 = 4$, the Basis Theorem states that the Hermite polynomials form a basis for $\mathbb{P}_3$.

Problem 29

Problem: True/False: If $S = \{\mathbf{v}_1, \dots, \mathbf{v}_p\}$ spans $V$, then $\dim V \leq p$.

Solution

True. Apply the Spanning Set Theorem to $S$ to produce a basis for $V$. This basis will not have more than $p$ elements, so $\dim V \leq p$.
