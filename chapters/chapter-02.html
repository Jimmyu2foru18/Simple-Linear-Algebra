Detailed Topic Breakdowns

Gaussian Elimination

Gaussian elimination is a systematic algorithm for solving systems of linear equations. The method transforms the augmented matrix into upper triangular form using elementary row operations, making the system easy to solve by back substitution.

Elementary Row Operations

Three types of row operations preserve the solution set:

  • Type I: Swap two rows ($R_i \leftrightarrow R_j$)
  • Type II: multiply a row by a nonzero scalar ($R_i \leftarrow c \cdot R_i$)
  • Type III: Add a multiple of one row to another ($R_i \leftarrow R_i + c \cdot R_j$)

Augmented Matrix

The augmented matrix combines the coefficient matrix $A$ and the right-hand side vector $\mathbf{b}$ into a single matrix $[A \mid \mathbf{b}]$. Row operations are applied to the entire augmented matrix, maintaining equivalence between the systems.

Back Substitution

Once the matrix is in upper triangular form, we solve for variables starting from the last equation (which has only one unknown) and substituting upward through the system.

Pivot and Elimination

A pivot is the first nonzero entry in a row during elimination. The pivot column determines which variable becomes a basic variable. Variables without pivots become free variables, indicating special solutions to homogeneous systems.

Computation Examples

Derivation: Row Operations as Matrix Multiplication

Each elementary row operation can be represented as left-multiplication by an elementary matrix. For example, swapping rows 1 and 2:

$$E = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b & c \\ d & e & f \\ g & h & i \end{bmatrix}, \quad EA = \begin{bmatrix} d & e & f \\ a & b & c \\ g & h & i \end{bmatrix}$$

Elementary matrices are invertible, and their inverses correspond to the reverse row operations.

Derivation: The Elimination Matrix $E$

For a $2 \times 2$ system, subtracting $2 \times$ row 1 from row 2 uses the elimination matrix:

$$E = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \text{Applying to } \begin{bmatrix} a & b & | & e \\ c & d & | & f \end{bmatrix}$$

Result:

$$\begin{bmatrix} a & b & | & e \\ c-2a & d-2b & | & f-2e \end{bmatrix}$$

The entry $-2$ is the multiplier $\ell_{21} = c/a$ that eliminates the $(2,1)$ entry.

Learning Strategies

Mnemonic Devices

Mnemonic: "Zero Below, Solve Above"

When performing elimination, create zeros below each pivot (going down columns). When back-substituting, solve from the bottom up (going up rows). This two-phase process ensures systematic solution.

Mnemonic: "Pivot First, Eliminate Rest"

For each column, identify the pivot first, then eliminate all entries below it before moving to the next column. This keeps the process organized and prevents errors.

Study Tips

  • Always write out the augmented matrix clearly before starting elimination
  • Track row operations carefully - one mistake propagates through the solution
  • Check your answer by substituting back into the original equations
  • Practice both the row-echelon and reduced row-echelon forms
  • Note when row exchanges are needed (zero pivots)

Worked Examples

Example 1: 2x2 System

Problem: Solve the system using Gaussian elimination.

$$2x + y = 5$$ $$4x - 6y = -2$$

Solution:

  1. Augmented matrix: $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & | & 5 \\ 4 & -6 & | & -2 \end{bmatrix}$
  2. Eliminate below pivot: $R_2 - 2R_1 \to R_2$
  3. Result: $\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 & | & 5 \\ 0 & -8 & | & -12 \end{bmatrix}$
  4. Back substitution: $y = (-12)/(-8) = 3/2 = 1.5$
  5. Substitute: $2x + 1.5 = 5 \Rightarrow 2x = 3.5 \Rightarrow x = 1.75$

Example 2: 3x3 System

Problem: Solve the system below.

$$x + y + z = 6$$ $$2x - y + z = 1$$ $$x + 2y + 3z = 14$$

Solution:

  1. Augmented matrix: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & | & 6 \\ 2 & -1 & 1 & | & 1 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 & | & 14 \end{bmatrix}$
  2. $R_2 - 2R_1$: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & | & 6 \\ 0 & -3 & -1 & | & -11 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 & | & 14 \end{bmatrix}$
  3. $R_3 - R_1$: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & | & 6 \\ 0 & -3 & -1 & | & -11 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & | & 8 \end{bmatrix}$
  4. Swap $R_2,R_3$: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & | & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & | & 8 \\ 0 & -3 & -1 & | & -11 \end{bmatrix}$
  5. $R_3 + 3R_2$: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 1 & | & 6 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & | & 8 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 & | & 13 \end{bmatrix}$
  6. Back sub: $z = 13/5 = 2.6$, $y = 8 - 2(2.6) = 2.8$, $x = 6 - 2.8 - 2.6 = 0.6$

Example 3: Singular System

Problem: Analyze the system and determine if a solution exists.

$$x + y + z = 1$$ $$2x + 2y + 2z = 2$$ $$x - y + z = 3$$

Analysis:

  • Row 2 = $2 \times$ Row 1 (dependent equations)
  • After elimination: $R_2 - 2R_1 = [0 \; 0 \; 0 \; | \; 0]$
  • Reduced system: $x + y + z = 1$ and $x - y + z = 3$
  • Subtracting: $2y = -2 \Rightarrow y = -1$
  • From first equation: $x + z = 2$
  • Infinitely many solutions: Parameterize $z = t$, then $x = 2 - t$, $y = -1$

Practice Problems

Problem Set 2

Problem 1: Solve by Gaussian elimination:

$$3x - y = 5$$ $$6x - 2y = 11$$

Hint: Check if the equations are consistent before starting elimination.

Solution

The second equation is $2 \times (3x - y) = 2 \times 5 = 10$, but the RHS is 11. Since $10 \neq 11$, the lines are parallel. No solution.

Problem 2: Solve the system and verify your answer:

$$x + 2y - z = 3$$ $$2x - y + 3z = 7$$ $$-x + 3y + 2z = 4$$

Hint: Write the augmented matrix first, then eliminate column by column.

Solution

Augmented: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & -1 & | & 3 \\ 2 & -1 & 3 & | & 7 \\ -1 & 3 & 2 & | & 4 \end{bmatrix}$

$R_2 - 2R_1$: $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -5 & 5 & | & 1 \end{bmatrix}$

$R_3 + R_1$: $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 5 & 1 & | & 7 \end{bmatrix}$

$R_3 + R_2$: $\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 6 & | & 8 \end{bmatrix}$ → $z = 4/3$

From $R_2$: $-5y + 5(4/3) = 1$ → $y = 17/15$

From $R_1$: $x + 2(17/15) - 4/3 = 3$ → $x = 31/15$

Solution: $x = 31/15$, $y = 17/15$, $z = 4/3$

Verification: $31/15 + 2(17/15) - 4/3 = 3$ ✓, $2(31/15) - 17/15 + 3(4/3) = 7$ ✓, $-31/15 + 3(17/15) + 2(4/3) = 4$ ✓

Problem 3: For the system below, find all solutions by reducing to RREF:

$$2x + y - z = 0$$ $$x + 2y + z = 3$$ $$3x + 3y = 3$$

Hint: Use RREF to identify pivot and free variables.

Solution

RREF yields: $x - z = -1$, $y + z = 2$, $0 = 0$

Solution: $x = -1 + z$, $y = 2 - z$ where $z$ is free.

Infinite solutions parameterized by $z$.

Problem 4: Determine whether the coefficient matrix is invertible based on the elimination result.

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 2 & 5 & 4 \\ 1 & 4 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$$

Hint: A matrix is invertible if elimination produces $n$ pivots.

Solution

After elimination: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$

Three pivots = rank 3 = $n$. A is invertible.
