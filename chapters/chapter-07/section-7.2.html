What is this section about?

What is a quadratic form? A quadratic form Q(x) = x⁺Ax can be diagonalized using an orthogonal change of variables, revealing its geometric behavior through the eigenvalues of A.

Core Lecture, Gilbert Strang (MIT)

Concept Overview

A quadratic form in variables $x_1, \dots, x_n$ is a function $Q: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}$ of the form:

$$Q(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{x}^T A \mathbf{x}$$

where $A$ is an $n \times n$ symmetric matrix.

A quadratic form is:

  • Positive definite: $Q(\mathbf{x}) > 0$ for all $\mathbf{x} \neq \mathbf{0}$
  • Negative definite: $Q(\mathbf{x}) < 0$ for all $\mathbf{x} \neq \mathbf{0}$
  • Indefinite: $Q(\mathbf{x})$ takes both positive and negative values

The principal axes theorem uses an orthogonal change of variables $\mathbf{x} = P\mathbf{y}$ to diagonalize the quadratic form:

$$Q(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{y}^T D \mathbf{y} = \lambda_1 y_1^2 + \cdots + \lambda_n y_n^2$$
