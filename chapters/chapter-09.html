Detailed Topic Breakdowns

Linear Independence

A set of vectors $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_k\}$ is linearly independent if the only solution to $c_1\mathbf{v}_1 + c_2\mathbf{v}_2 + \cdots + c_k\mathbf{v}_k = \mathbf{0}$ is $c_1 = c_2 = \cdots = c_k = 0$. If any nonzero coefficients produce zero, the vectors are dependent.

Span and Basis

The span of a set of vectors is all possible linear combinations. A basis for a vector space $V$ is a set of vectors that are both linearly independent and span $V$. Every vector in $V$ can be written uniquely as a linear combination of basis vectors.

Dimension

The dimension of a vector space is the number of vectors in any basis for that space. All bases for a given space have the same number of vectors. $\dim(\mathbb{R}^n) = n$. The dimension of a subspace is less than or equal to the dimension of the parent space.

Rank and Matrix Spaces

The rank of a matrix is the dimension of its column space (or row space). The rank equals the number of pivot columns. It measures how much "information" the matrix preserves.

Computation Examples

Derivation: Independence Test via Matrix

To test if vectors $\mathbf{v}_1, \mathbf{v}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_k$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ are independent:

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{v}_1 & \mathbf{v}_2 & \cdots & \mathbf{v}_k \end{bmatrix}$$ $$A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$$

If only trivial solution $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$ exists: independent. If nontrivial solution exists: dependent.

The vectors are independent exactly when $\text{rank}(A) = k$.

Derivation: Uniqueness of Basis Representation

If $\{\mathbf{v}_1, \ldots, \mathbf{v}_n\}$ is a basis for $V$ and $\mathbf{v} = c_1\mathbf{v}_1 + \cdots + c_n\mathbf{v}_n = d_1\mathbf{v}_1 + \cdots + d_n\mathbf{v}_n$, then:

$$(c_1 - d_1)\mathbf{v}_1 + \cdots + (c_n - d_n)\mathbf{v}_n = \mathbf{0}$$ $$\text{By independence: } c_1 - d_1 = 0, \ldots, c_n - d_n = 0$$ $$\text{Therefore: } c_i = d_i \text{ for all } i$$

Basis representations are unique.

Learning Strategies

Mnemonic Devices

Mnemonic: "INDEPENDENT = No Empty Weight"

A set is independent if no vector in the set can be written as a combination of the others. Like people: if no one's weight comes from standing on others' shoulders, they're independent.

Mnemonic: "Basis = Just Right"

A basis has exactly the right number of vectors: enough to span the space, but not so many that they become dependent. Not too few (can't span), not too many (dependent).

Study Tips

  • Always test independence by solving $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$ where $A$ has the vectors as columns
  • More vectors than dimensions ($k > n$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$) always means dependence
  • The standard basis for $\mathbb{R}^n$ is $\mathbf{e}_1, \mathbf{e}_2, \ldots, \mathbf{e}_n$
  • To extend a linearly independent set to a basis, add vectors from the standard basis that aren't already in the span
  • Dimension is invariant — all bases have the same size

Worked Examples

Example 1: Testing Independence

Problem: Determine if $\mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{v}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 7 \\ 10 \end{bmatrix}$ are independent.

Solution:

  1. Form $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 5 & 7 \\ 3 & 7 & 10 \end{bmatrix}$
  2. RREF: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
  3. Rank = 2 $< 3$ (number of vectors)
  4. Nontrivial solution exists: $\mathbf{v}_3 = \mathbf{v}_1 + \mathbf{v}_2$
  5. Vectors are dependent.

Example 2: Finding a Basis

Problem: Find a basis for the subspace $S = \text{span}\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\}$.

Solution:

  • Note: $\mathbf{v}_2 = 2\mathbf{v}_1$, so $\mathbf{v}_2$ is dependent on $\mathbf{v}_1$
  • Check if $\mathbf{v}_3$ is independent of $\mathbf{v}_1$: not a multiple
  • RREF of $[\mathbf{v}_1 \; \mathbf{v}_2 \; \mathbf{v}_3]$ gives pivots in columns 1 and 3
  • Basis: $\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\}$
  • $\dim(S) = 2$

Practice Problems

Problem Set 9

Problem 1: Are the vectors $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ in $\mathbb{R}^3$ independent?

Hint: Three vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$ can be independent if they form a basis.

Solution

RREF of $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = I$. Rank = 3. Independent.

Problem 2: What is the dimension of the subspace of $\mathbb{R}^4$ spanned by $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$?

Hint: Compute the rank of the matrix with these vectors as columns.

Solution

The four vectors are independent (RREF = I). Dimension = 4. They span all of $\mathbb{R}^4$.

Problem 3: Extend $\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\}$ to a basis for $\mathbb{R}^2$.

Hint: Add any vector not in the span of the first vector.

Solution

Add $\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$ or $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$ or any vector not equal to $c\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$. Basis: $\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\}$.

Problem 4: If a subspace $S$ of $\mathbb{R}^5$ has dimension 3, what is the maximum number of linearly independent vectors it can contain?

Hint: Think about the definition of dimension.

Solution

3. Any set of 4 or more vectors in a 3-dimensional subspace must be dependent.
