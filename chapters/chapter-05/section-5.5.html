What is this section about?

What are complex eigenvalues? A real 2×2 matrix with complex eigenvalues a ± bi is similar to a rotation-scaling matrix C = [[a, −b], [b, a]], representing rotation and scaling.

Core Lecture, Gilbert Strang (MIT)

Concept Overview

A real $2 \times 2$ matrix with complex eigenvalues $\lambda = a \pm bi$ (where $b \neq 0$) is similar to a rotation-scaling matrix:

$$A = PCP^{-1}, \quad \text{where} \quad C = \begin{bmatrix} a & -b \\ b & a \end{bmatrix}$$

Here $a = \text{Re}(\lambda)$ and $b = |\text{Im}(\lambda)|$. The matrix $C$ represents a rotation by angle $\theta$ and scaling by $r = |\lambda| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$, where $\tan\theta = b/a$.

The columns of $P$ are constructed from the real and imaginary parts of a complex eigenvector.
