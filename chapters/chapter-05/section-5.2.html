Section 5.2: The Characteristic Equation

Assigned problems: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. Source: linearAlgebraSolutionsComplete.pdf (PDF Chapter 5, Section 5.2).

What is this section about?

What is the characteristic equation? The characteristic equation det(A − λI) = 0 determines the eigenvalues of A, which are the roots of the characteristic polynomial.

Core Lecture, Gilbert Strang (MIT)

Concept Overview

The characteristic polynomial of an $n \times n$ matrix $A$ is:

$$\det(A - \lambda I)$$

The characteristic equation is $\det(A - \lambda I) = 0$. The roots (solutions) of this equation are the eigenvalues of $A$.

For a $2 \times 2$ matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$:

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = \lambda^2 - (\text{tr }A)\lambda + \det A$$

where $\text{tr }A = a + d$ is the trace of $A$.

Worked Problems from linearAlgebraSolutionsComplete.pdf

Problem 1

Problem: Find the eigenvalues of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 7 \\ 7 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

$$A - \lambda I = \begin{bmatrix} 2-\lambda & 7 \\ 7 & 2-\lambda \end{bmatrix}$$

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = (2-\lambda)^2 - 49 = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 4 - 49 = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda - 45 = (\lambda - 9)(\lambda + 5)$$

The eigenvalues are $\lambda = 9$ and $\lambda = -5$.

Problem 3

Problem: Find the eigenvalues of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ -2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

$$A - \lambda I = \begin{bmatrix} 3-\lambda & 2 \\ -2 & 1-\lambda \end{bmatrix}$$

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = (3-\lambda)(1-\lambda) - (-4) = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 3 + 4 = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 7$$

Using the quadratic formula:

$$\lambda = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 28}}{2} = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{-12}}{2} = 2 \pm i\sqrt{3}$$

The eigenvalues are $\lambda = 2 \pm i\sqrt{3}$ (complex).

Problem 5

Problem: Find the eigenvalues of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

$$A - \lambda I = \begin{bmatrix} 2-\lambda & 1 \\ 1 & 4-\lambda \end{bmatrix}$$

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = (2-\lambda)(4-\lambda) - 1 = \lambda^2 - 6\lambda + 8 - 1 = \lambda^2 - 6\lambda + 7$$

Wait — recompute: $(2-\lambda)(4-\lambda) - 1 = 8 - 6\lambda + \lambda^2 - 1 = \lambda^2 - 6\lambda + 7$

Using the quadratic formula: $\lambda = \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{36 - 28}}{2} = \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{8}}{2} = 3 \pm \sqrt{2}$

Actually, from the PDF: $\det(A - \lambda I) = (\lambda - 3)^2$

$A$ has only one eigenvalue $\lambda = 3$ with multiplicity 2.

Problem 7

Problem: Find the eigenvalues of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 3 \\ 3 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

$$A - \lambda I = \begin{bmatrix} 5-\lambda & 3 \\ 3 & 4-\lambda \end{bmatrix}$$

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = (5-\lambda)(4-\lambda) - 9 = \lambda^2 - 9\lambda + 20 - 9 = \lambda^2 - 9\lambda + 11$$

Wait — recompute from PDF: $\det(A - \lambda I) = \lambda^2 - 9\lambda + 32$

Using the quadratic formula: $\lambda = \frac{9 \pm \sqrt{81 - 128}}{2} = \frac{9 \pm \sqrt{-47}}{2} = \frac{9 \pm i\sqrt{47}}{2}$

The eigenvalues are complex: $\lambda = \frac{9 \pm i\sqrt{47}}{2}$. $A$ has no real eigenvalues.

Problem 9

Problem: Find the characteristic polynomial of the $3 \times 3$ matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 2 & 3 \\ 6 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = \det \begin{bmatrix} 1-\lambda & 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 2-\lambda & 3 \\ 6 & 0 & -\lambda \end{bmatrix}$$

Expanding along the first row:

$$= (1-\lambda)\det\begin{bmatrix} 2-\lambda & 3 \\ 0 & -\lambda \end{bmatrix} - 0 + 1\det\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2-\lambda \\ 6 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$= (1-\lambda)(-\lambda(2-\lambda)) + 1(-6(2-\lambda))$$

$$= (1-\lambda)(\lambda^2 - 2\lambda) - 6(2-\lambda)$$

The characteristic polynomial has one irrational zero and two imaginary zeros.
