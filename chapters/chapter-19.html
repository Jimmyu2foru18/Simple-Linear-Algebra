Detailed Topic Breakdowns

Overview

This chapter introduces the cofactor formula, a recursive method for computing determinants of any $n \times n$ matrix by expanding along a row or column. Rather than using the $n!$ term big formula or Gaussian elimination to pivots, cofactor expansion breaks the determinant down into smaller determinants of size $(n-1) \times (n-1)$, building up from $1 \times 1$ cases.

Key Concepts

  • Expanding along a row: Each element $a_{ij}$ in a row contributes $a_{ij}$ times its cofactor $C_{ij}$ to the determinant.
  • Checkerboard sign pattern: The cofactor of $a_{ij}$ carries a sign $(-1)^{i+j}$, producing a $+$ / $-$ / $+$ / $-$ alternating pattern across both rows and columns.
  • Minor vs. cofactor: The minor $M_{ij}$ is the determinant of the submatrix with row $i$ and column $j$ removed; the cofactor $C_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j} M_{ij}$ includes the sign.
  • Tri-diagonal example: A recurrence $\det(A_n) = \det(A_{n-1}) - \det(A_{n-2})$ yields a periodic sequence of period six.

Computation Examples

Formal Definition: Minors and Cofactors

For an $n \times n$ matrix $A$, the minor $M_{ij}$ of entry $a_{ij}$ is the determinant of the $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ matrix that remains after deleting row $i$ and column $j$. The cofactor is:

$$C_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j}\, M_{ij} = (-1)^{i+j} \det(A \text{ with row } i \text{ and column } j \text{ removed})$$

The sign $(-1)^{i+j}$ depends only on position:

  • If $i+j$ is even, the cofactor is $+M_{ij}$.
  • If $i+j$ is odd, the cofactor is $-M_{ij}$.

Written as a checkerboard for a $4 \times 4$ matrix:

$$\begin{bmatrix} + & - & + & - \\ - & + & - & + \\ + & - & + & - \\ - & + & - & + \end{bmatrix}$$

Row $1$ starts with $+$, row $2$ starts with $-$, row $3$ starts with $+$, and so on.

The Cofactor Formula

Expanding along row $i$:

$$\det(A) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}\, C_{ij} = a_{i1}C_{i1} + a_{i2}C_{i2} + \cdots + a_{in}C_{in}$$

Expanding along the first row specifically:

$$\det(A) = a_{11}C_{11} + a_{12}C_{12} + a_{13}C_{13} + \cdots + a_{1n}C_{1n}$$

Each cofactor $C_{1j}$ is the determinant of an $(n-1) \times (n-1)$ matrix times $(-1)^{1+j}$, so this reduces an $n \times n$ determinant to $n$ determinants of size $(n-1)$.

Boxed result (cofactor expansion along row $i$):

$$\boxed{\det(A) = \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij}\, C_{ij}}$$

Learning Strategies

Mnemonic Devices

Mnemonic: "Cofactor = Minor with a Sign"

The cofactor $C_{ij}$ equals the minor $M_{ij}$ times $(-1)^{i+j}$. The sign depends only on position: even $i+j$ gives $+$, odd $i+j$ gives $-$. This creates the checkerboard pattern.

Mnemonic: "Expand Along the Row with Most Zeros"

Cofactor expansion is fastest when you pick a row or column with the most zeros. Each zero entry contributes $0 \times C_{ij} = 0$, eliminating entire terms from the sum.

Mnemonic: "Determinant of Transpose = Determinant of Original"

Expanding along a column gives the same result as expanding along a row because $\det(A) = \det(A^T)$. The cofactor formula works identically for rows and columns.

Study Tips

  • Choose the row or column with the most zeros for cofactor expansion — zero entries eliminate entire terms.
  • Memorize the checkerboard pattern: $(+,-,+,-,\ldots)$ for row 1, $(-,+,-,+,\ldots)$ for row 2.
  • After expanding, you get a recursion. Practice the base case until it is automatic.
  • Always verify a $2 \times 2$ result against $ad - bc$ as a sanity check.

Visual Aids

The cofactor of $a_{ij}$ is multiplied by $(-1)^{i+j}$, which creates a checkerboard of $+$ and $-$ signs. Expanding along any row or column uses this same pattern scaled to the matrix size.

$$+ \;\;\;- \;\;\;+ \;\;\;- \;\;\;\cdots$$

$$- \;\;\;+ \;\;\;- \;\;\;+ \;\;\;\cdots$$

Row $i$ begins with $+$ when $i$ is odd (since $i + 1$ is even) and with $-$ when $i$ is even.

Figure: Tri-Diagonal Matrix $A_n$

The $n \times n$ tri-diagonal matrix of ones has $1$'s on the main diagonal, the first super-diagonal, and the first sub-diagonal, and $0$'s elsewhere:

$$A_4 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Expanding along the first row gives $\det(A_n) = \det(A_{n-1}) - \det(A_{n-2})$, a simple two-term recurrence.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Two by Two Formula

Problem: Use the cofactor formula to compute $\det(A)$ for $A = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}$ and confirm it equals $ad - bc$.

Solution:

  1. Expand along row 1: $\det(A) = a \cdot C_{11} + b \cdot C_{12}$.
  2. Compute $C_{11}$: delete row 1, column 1, leaving $[d]$, so $M_{11} = d$ and $(-1)^{1+1} = +1$. Thus $C_{11} = d$.
  3. Compute $C_{12}$: delete row 1, column 2, leaving $[c]$, so $M_{12} = c$ and $(-1)^{1+2} = -1$. Thus $C_{12} = -c$.
  4. Combine: $\det(A) = a \cdot d + b \cdot (-c) = ad - bc$. $\checkmark$

Example 2: Three by Three Determinant

Problem: Compute $\det(A_3)$ for the tri-diagonal matrix $A_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution — Method 1 (cofactor expansion):

Expand along row 1:

$$\det(A_3) = 1 \cdot \underbrace{\det\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}}_{= 0} - 1 \cdot \underbrace{\det\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix}}_{= 1} + 0 \cdot (\cdots) = -1$$

Solution — Method 2 (row operations):

Subtract row 3 from row 2 (determinant unchanged):

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1 & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \xrightarrow{R_2 \to R_2 - R_3} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Now expand along row 2 (which has two zeros): only the $(2,1)$ entry survives. Its minor is obtained by deleting row 2 and column 1, giving $\det\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 \\ 1 & 1\end{bmatrix} = 1$. Thus $\det(A_3) = 1 \cdot (-1)^{2+1} \cdot 1 = -1$. Both methods agree: $\det(A_3) = -1$.

Example 3: Four by Four — The Recurrence

Problem: Find the recurrence for $\det(A_n)$ and compute $\det(A_n)$ for $n = 4, 5, 6, 7$.

Solution:

Expand $\det(A_n)$ along the first row. The first entry is $a_{11} = 1$ (a $+1$ cofactor), and the second entry is $a_{12} = 1$ (a $-1$ cofactor). All other entries in row 1 are $0$, so only two terms survive:

$$\det(A_n) = 1 \cdot \det(A_{n-1}) - 1 \cdot \det(A_{n-2})$$ $$\boxed{\det(A_n) = \det(A_{n-1}) - \det(A_{n-2})}$$

This holds for all $n \geq 3$. The base cases are $\det(A_1) = 1$ and $\det(A_2) = 0$.

Now iterate:

$n$Recurrence$\det(A_n)$
1(base case)$1$
2(base case)$0$
3$0 - 1$$-1$
4$-1 - 0$$-1$
5$-1 - (-1)$$0$
6$0 - (-1)$$1$
7$1 - 0$$1$

The sequence is $\;1,\; 0,\; -1,\; -1,\; 0,\; 1,\; 1,\; 0,\; -1,\; -1,\; \ldots\;$ and it repeats with period 6. For example, $\det(A_{61}) = \det(A_1) = 1$ since $61 \equiv 1 \pmod{6}$.

Practice Problems

Problem Set

Problem 1: Compute the determinant of $\begin{bmatrix} 3 & -2 \\ 5 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$ using the cofactor formula along the first row.

Hint: The cofactor of the $(1,1)$ entry is $+4$; the cofactor of the $(1,2)$ entry is $-5$.

Solution

$\det = 3 \cdot 4 + (-2) \cdot (-5) = 12 + 10 = 22.$

Problem 2: For $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 & 3 \\ 0 & -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}$, expand along the first row to find $\det(A)$.

Hint: Since $a_{12} = 0$, only two cofactors are needed: $C_{11}$ and $C_{13}$.

Solution

$\det(A) = 2 \cdot \det\begin{bmatrix}1&3\\-1&2\end{bmatrix} - 0 \cdot (\cdots) + 1 \cdot \det\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&-1\end{bmatrix} = 2(2-(-3)) + 1(-1-0) = 2 \cdot 5 - 1 = 9.$

Problem 3: Compute $\det(A_6)$ for the $6 \times 6$ tri-diagonal matrix of ones defined in Example 3.

Hint: Use the recurrence $\det(A_n) = \det(A_{n-1}) - \det(A_{n-2})$ and the values from the table.

Solution

From the table: $\det(A_5) = 0$ and $\det(A_4) = -1$, so $\det(A_6) = 0 - (-1) = 1.$

Problem 4: In the cofactor expansion $\det(A) = \sum_{j} a_{ij} C_{ij}$ along row $i$, explain why you can also expand along a column: $\det(A) = \sum_{i} a_{ij} C_{ij}$.

Hint: Recall that $\det(A) = \det(A^T)$ — the transpose swaps rows and columns.

Solution

Since $\det(A) = \det(A^T)$, expanding $A^T$ along its row $j$ gives $\det(A) = \sum_i a_{ji}^{(T)} C_{ji}^{(T)}$. But entry $(j,i)$ of $A^T$ is $a_{ij}$ of $A$, and its cofactor equals the cofactor of $a_{ij}$ in $A$ (the submatrix is the same). Hence $\det(A) = \sum_i a_{ij} C_{ij}$, expanding along column $j$.

Problem 5: True or false: If you expand a $3 \times 3$ determinant along a row that contains two zeros, you only need to compute one $2 \times 2$ determinant.

Hint: Each zero entry contributes $0 \times C_{ij} = 0$ to the sum.

Solution

True. If a row has two zeros, two of the three terms $a_{ij}C_{ij}$ vanish, leaving only the one term from the non-zero entry.
