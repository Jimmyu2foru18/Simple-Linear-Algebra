What is this section about?

What is a linear transformation? A linear transformation T: V → W between vector spaces preserves addition and scalar multiplication. Its kernel is the set mapped to zero; its range is the set of all outputs.

Core Lecture, Gilbert Strang (MIT)

Concept Overview

A linear transformation $T: V \to W$ between vector spaces $V$ and $W$ satisfies:

  • $T(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = T(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v})$ for all $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}$ in $V$
  • $T(c\mathbf{u}) = cT(\mathbf{u})$ for all $\mathbf{u}$ in $V$ and scalars $c$

The kernel of $T$ is $\ker(T) = \{\mathbf{v} \in V : T(\mathbf{v}) = \mathbf{0}\}$.

The range of $T$ is $\text{range}(T) = \{T(\mathbf{v}) : \mathbf{v} \in V\}$.
