Chapter 6: Column Space and Nullspace

Detailed Topic Breakdowns

The Column Space $C(A)$

The column space of $A$, denoted $C(A)$, is the set of all linear combinations of the columns of $A$. It is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^m$ (where $A$ is $m \times n$). The system $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ is solvable if and only if $\mathbf{b}$ lies in $C(A)$.

The Nullspace $N(A)$

The nullspace of $A$, denoted $N(A)$, is the set of all solutions to $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$. It is a subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$. If $A$ is square and invertible, the nullspace contains only the zero vector. Otherwise, the nullspace has nontrivial solutions.

Rank and Pivot Columns

The rank of $A$ is the dimension of the column space, equal to the number of pivot columns in the row-echelon form of $A$. The pivot columns of $A$ form a basis for $C(A)$.

Free Variables and the Nullspace

Variables corresponding to non-pivot columns are free variables. Setting one free variable to 1 (and others to 0) generates a special solution. All special solutions span the nullspace.

Computation Examples

Derivation: When is $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ Solvable?

$A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ has a solution if and only if $\mathbf{b}$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$. This means $\mathbf{b}$ must be orthogonal to every vector in the left nullspace of $A$ (every row of the reduced matrix $[A \mid \mathbf{b}]$ must have the same rank as $A$).

$$\text{Condition: } \text{rank}([A \mid \mathbf{b}]) = \text{rank}(A)$$

Derivation: Rank-Nullity Theorem (Preview)

For an $m \times n$ matrix $A$ with rank $r$:

$$\dim(C(A)) = r \quad \text{(number of pivot columns)}$$ $$\dim(N(A)) = n - r \quad \text{(number of free variables)}$$ $$\dim(C(A)) + \dim(N(A)) = n \quad \text{(Rank-Nullity Theorem)}$$

This fundamental relationship connects the column space and nullspace dimensions.

Learning Strategies

Mnemonic Devices

Mnemonic: "C is for Column, N is for Null"

The column space $C(A)$ answers: "What outputs can $A$ produce?" The nullspace $N(A)$ answers: "What inputs produce zero?" These are the two fundamental questions of linear algebra.

Mnemonic: "Pivot = Basic, Free = Special"

Pivot columns correspond to basic variables (determined by the system). Non-pivot columns correspond to free variables (parameters for special solutions in the nullspace).

Study Tips

  • Always find the RREF to identify pivot columns and free variables
  • Pivot columns of $A$ (not RREF) form a basis for $C(A)$
  • Special solutions are found by setting one free variable to 1 at a time
  • The nullspace always contains the zero vector
  • Practice switching between matrix notation and span notation

Visual Aids

Figure: Column Space in $\mathbb{R}^3$

For $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 9 \end{bmatrix}$, the column space is a plane through the origin in $\mathbb{R}^3$ because column 3 = $-1 \cdot$ column 1 $+ 2 \cdot$ column 2 (linearly dependent).

$$\mathbf{c}_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{c}_2 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 5 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix}, \quad \mathbf{c}_3 = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 6 \\ 9 \end{bmatrix} = -1 \cdot \mathbf{c}_1 + 2 \cdot \mathbf{c}_2$$

Basis for $C(A)$: $\{\mathbf{c}_1, \mathbf{c}_2\}$. Dimension = 2.

Figure: Nullspace as Solutions to $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$

The nullspace is the set of all $\mathbf{x}$ that $A$ "collapses" to zero. For a $3 \times 3$ matrix of rank 2, the nullspace is a line through the origin in $\mathbb{R}^3$.

$$\text{Special solutions span } N(A):$$ $$\text{For free variable } x_3: \quad \mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$ $$N(A) = \{ c \begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} : c \in \mathbb{R} \}$$

Worked Examples

Example 1: Finding Column Space

Problem: Find a basis and dimension for $C(A)$ where:

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 & 8 \\ 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Solution:

  1. RREF of $A$: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$
  2. Pivot columns: 1 and 2
  3. Basis for $C(A)$: columns 1 and 2 of $A$: $\{\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}\}$
  4. $\dim(C(A)) = \text{rank}(A) = 2$

Example 2: Finding the Nullspace

Problem: Describe the nullspace of $A$ from Example 1.

Solution:

  1. RREF: $x_1 + x_3 = 0$, $x_2 + x_3 + 2x_4 = 0$
  2. Free variables: $x_3, x_4$
  3. Special solution 1 ($x_3 = 1, x_4 = 0$): $\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ -1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$
  4. Special solution 2 ($x_3 = 0, x_4 = 1$): $\begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$
  5. Basis for $N(A)$: $\{\begin{bmatrix} -1 \\ -1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}, \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ -2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}\}$
  6. $\dim(N(A)) = 2$

Practice Problems

Problem Set 6

Problem 1: For $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 2 \\ 2 & 6 & 4 \end{bmatrix}$, determine whether $\mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$ is in $C(A)$.

Hint: Solve $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ or check if $\mathbf{b}$ is a combination of columns.

Solution

Columns 2 and 3 are multiples of column 1. $C(A)$ is the line through $\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$. Since $\mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$ is exactly column 1, yes, $\mathbf{b} \in C(A)$.

Problem 2: Find a basis for the nullspace of $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 2 & 4 & 6 \end{bmatrix}$.

Hint: RREF reveals the free variables.

Solution

RREF: $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$

Rank = 1, so 2 free variables: $x_2, x_3$ are free.

Special solutions: $\begin{bmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix} -3 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$

Problem 3: Explain why the column space of $A^T$ is the row space of $A$.

Hint: Think about what the columns of $A^T$ are.

Solution

The columns of $A^T$ are the rows of $A$. So $C(A^T) = \text{span}(\text{rows of } A) = \text{row space of } A$.

Problem 4: Can a $3 \times 4$ matrix have its column space equal to $\mathbb{R}^3$? Explain.

Hint: How many pivot columns are needed to span $\mathbb{R}^3$?

Solution

Yes. If rank = 3, the three pivot columns span $\mathbb{R}^3$. A $3 \times 4$ matrix can have rank 3 (if columns 1, 2, 3 are independent).
