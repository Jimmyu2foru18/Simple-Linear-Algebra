Detailed Topic Breakdowns

The Diagonalization Formula

If A is diagonalizable with eigenvector matrix S and eigenvalue matrix Î›, then A = SÎ›S-1. Consequently, Ak = SÎ›kS-1. This formula means that to compute Ak, you expand the initial vector u0 into eigenvectors, multiply each eigencomponent by its eigenvalue raised to the k-th power, and recombine. The matrix S contains the eigenvectors as columns, and Î› is the diagonal matrix of eigenvalues.

From Recurrence to Dynamical Systems

The Fibonacci sequence satisfies Fk+2 = Fk+1 + Fk, which is a second-order scalar recurrence. To convert it to a first-order system, define the state vector uk = [Fk+1, Fk]T. Then uk+1 = Auk where A = [[1, 1], [1, 0]]. This transforms a single second-order equation into a 2Ã—2 matrix system. The matrix A captures the recurrence in first-order form.

For A = [[1, 1], [1, 0]], the characteristic equation is det(A - Î»I) = Î»2 - Î» - 1 = 0. The eigenvalues are Î»1 = (1+âˆš5)/2 â‰ˆ 1.618 and Î»2 = (1-âˆš5)/2 â‰ˆ -0.618. They add to the trace (1) and multiply to the determinant (-1). The dominant eigenvalue Î»1 is the golden ratio, and it controls the exponential growth of the Fibonacci numbers. The second eigenvalue Î»2 has magnitude less than 1, so its contribution vanishes as k increases.

Eigenvectors and the Expansion of u0

The eigenvectors of A are x1 = [Î»1, 1]T and x2 = [Î»2, 1]T. The initial vector u0 = [F1, F0]T = [1, 0]T is expressed as a linear combination u0 = c1x1 + c2x2. Then uk = Aku0 = c1Î»1kx1 + c2Î»2kx2. As k grows, the term with |Î»1|k dominates because |Î»1| > 1 and |Î»2| < 1.

Dynamic Systems vs Static Problems

Static problems solve Ax = b for a fixed right-hand side. Dynamic problems follow uk+1 = Auk over time. The eigenvalues determine the long-term behavior: if |Î»| < 1, solutions decay to zero; if |Î»| > 1, solutions grow exponentially; if |Î»| = 1, solutions remain bounded. For the Fibonacci matrix, Î»1 â‰ˆ 1.618 > 1 means exponential growth, and the growth rate is approximately Î»1k.

Computation Examples

Derivation: The Diagonalization Formula for Ak

If A has n independent eigenvectors, they form the columns of S = [x1 x2 ... xn]. Then A = SÎ›S-1 where Î› = diag(Î»1, Î»2, ..., Î»n). Raising to the k-th power:

$$ A^k = (S\Lambda S^{-1})^k = S\Lambda S^{-1} S\Lambda S^{-1} \cdots S\Lambda S^{-1} $$

Since S-1S = I in the middle, all interior terms cancel:

$$ A^k = S\Lambda^k S^{-1} $$

Here Î›k = diag(Î»1k, Î»2k, ..., Î»nk). This is the fundamental formula for matrix powers.

Derivation: Fibonacci Recurrence as a Matrix System

The scalar recurrence Fk+2 = Fk+1 + Fk with F0 = 0, F1 = 1 is converted to first-order form by defining uk = [Fk+1, Fk]T. Then:

$$ u_{k+1} = \begin{bmatrix} F_{k+2} \\ F_{k+1} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} F_{k+1} + F_k \\ F_{k+1} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} F_{k+1} \\ F_k \end{bmatrix} = Au_k $$

Thus uk = Aku0 with u0 = [1, 0]T. The Fibonacci numbers appear as components of uk: Fk is the second component of uk-1.

Derivation: Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors of the Fibonacci Matrix

For A = [[1, 1], [1, 0]], the characteristic equation is:

$$ \det(A - \lambda I) = \det\begin{pmatrix} 1-\lambda & 1 \\ 1 & -\lambda \end{pmatrix} = \lambda^2 - \lambda - 1 = 0 $$

Using the quadratic formula:

$$ \lambda_{1,2} = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2} $$

For each eigenvalue, the eigenvector satisfies (A - Î»I)x = 0. For a 2Ã—2 matrix, if Î» is an eigenvalue, then [Î», 1]T is an eigenvector because:

$$ \begin{pmatrix} 1-\lambda & 1 \\ 1 & -\lambda \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \lambda \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \lambda - \lambda^2 + 1 \\ \lambda - \lambda^2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} $$

since Î»2 - Î» - 1 = 0. Thus x1 = [Î»1, 1]T and x2 = [Î»2, 1]T.

Learning Strategies

Mnemonic Devices

Mnemonic: "A to the k = S Lambda to the k S inverse"

To compute Ak, diagonalize A = SÎ›S-1, then raise the diagonal matrix Î› to the k-th power entrywise, and sandwich it between S and S-1. The hard work is finding S and Î›; raising Î›k is trivial.

Mnemonic: "Second Order to First Order: Stack the State"

To convert a recurrence like Fk+2 = Fk+1 + Fk into a matrix system, define uk = [Fk+1, Fk]T. This stacks the current and previous terms into a vector, turning a scalar recurrence into a first-order system.

Mnemonic: "Dominant Eigenvalue Controls Growth"

For large k, the term with the largest |Î»| dominates Ak. If |Î»1| > |Î»2| > ... > |Î»n|, then Ak â‰ˆ Î»1k times a rank-1 matrix. The Fibonacci growth rate is Î»1 â‰ˆ 1.618.

Study Tips

  • Always check that A has n independent eigenvectors before applying Ak = SÎ›kS-1
  • For a 2Ã—2 matrix, the eigenvectors often have the simple form [Î», 1]T
  • The trace equals the sum of eigenvalues; the determinant equals the product - use these to check your work
  • When converting recurrences to matrix form, the state vector must contain enough history to make the system first-order
  • The dominant eigenvalue (largest magnitude) determines the long-term growth or decay rate
  • If all |Î»i| < 1, then Ak â†’ 0 as k â†’ âˆž
