Section 1.4: The Matrix Equation Ax = b

Assigned problems: 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 11, 15, 17, 21. Source: linearAlgebraSolutionsComplete.pdf.

What is this section about?

What is a matrix equation? The matrix equation Ax = b compactly represents a system of linear equations, where A is the coefficient matrix, x is the vector of unknowns, and b is the right-hand side vector.

Core Lecture, Gilbert Strang (MIT)

Concept Overview

If $A$ is an $m \times n$ matrix with columns $\mathbf{a}_1, \dots, \mathbf{a}_n$, and $\mathbf{x}$ is in $\mathbb{R}^n$, then:

$$A\mathbf{x} = x_1\mathbf{a}_1 + x_2\mathbf{a}_2 + \cdots + x_n\mathbf{a}_n$$

The equation $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ has a solution if and only if $\mathbf{b}$ is a linear combination of the columns of $A$.

Worked Problems from linearAlgebraSolutionsComplete.pdf

Problem 1

Problem: Is the matrix-vector product $A\mathbf{x}$ defined? $A$ is a $3 \times 2$ matrix and $\mathbf{x}$ has 3 entries.

Solution

The matrix-vector product $A\mathbf{x}$ is not defined because the number of columns in $A$ (2) does not match the number of entries in $\mathbf{x}$ (3).

Problem 3

Problem: Compute $A\mathbf{x}$ where $A = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 5 \\ 6 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$. Verify by computing as a linear combination of columns.

Solution

$$A\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 5 \\ 6 & 5 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = 1\begin{bmatrix} 6 \\ 6 \end{bmatrix} + 2\begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 6 + 10 \\ 6 + 10 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 16 \\ 16 \end{bmatrix}$$

Problem 4

Problem: Compute $A\mathbf{x}$ where $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 8 \\ 3 & 4 \\ 4 & 8 \end{bmatrix}$ and $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ -4 \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

$$A\mathbf{x} = 3\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 3 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} + (-4)\begin{bmatrix} 8 \\ 4 \\ 8 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 - 32 \\ 9 - 16 \\ 12 - 32 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -29 \\ -7 \\ -20 \end{bmatrix}$$

Problem 5

Problem: Write $A\mathbf{x}$ as a linear combination of the columns of $A$ using the entries of $\mathbf{x}$ as weights.

Solution

$$A\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 5 & 1 \\ 8 & 4 \\ 4 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 5 \end{bmatrix} = 3\begin{bmatrix} 5 \\ 8 \\ 4 \end{bmatrix} + 5\begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 15 + 5 \\ 24 + 20 \\ 12 + 10 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 20 \\ 44 \\ 22 \end{bmatrix}$$

Problem 6

Problem: Write $A\mathbf{x}$ as a linear combination of the columns of $A$ using the entries of $\mathbf{x}$ as weights.

Solution

$$A\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 3 \\ 2 & 1 \\ 3 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -9 \end{bmatrix} = 1\begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} + (-9)\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 7 - 27 \\ 2 - 9 \\ 3 - 18 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -20 \\ -7 \\ -15 \end{bmatrix}$$

Problem 10

Problem: Convert the vector equation to a matrix equation $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$.

Solution

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 8 \\ 5 & 4 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 & 2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \\ x_3 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 4 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

Problem 11

Problem: Solve $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ by row reducing the augmented matrix $[\mathbf{a}_1 \, \mathbf{a}_2 \, \mathbf{a}_3 \, | \, \mathbf{b}]$.

Solution

Row reducing the augmented matrix gives:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 & | & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 5 & | & 2 \\ 2 & 4 & 3 & | & 9 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & | & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & | & -3 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & | & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Solution: $\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -3 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$

Problem 15

Problem: For which $\mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix} b_1 \\ b_2 \\ b_3 \end{bmatrix}$ is $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ consistent?

Solution

Row reducing the augmented matrix shows that the equation is consistent if and only if $3b_1 + b_2 = 0$, i.e., $b_2 = -3b_1$.

The set of $\mathbf{b}$ for which the equation is consistent is the line $b_2 = -3b_1$ through the origin.

Problem 17

Problem: Does $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ have a solution for each $\mathbf{b}$ in $\mathbb{R}^4$?

Solution

Row reduction shows that only three rows of $A$ contain a pivot position. Since not every row contains a pivot, by Theorem 4, $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ does not have a solution for each $\mathbf{b}$ in $\mathbb{R}^4$.

Problem 21

Problem: Do the columns of $[\mathbf{v}_1 \, \mathbf{v}_2 \, \mathbf{v}_3]$ span $\mathbb{R}^4$?

Solution

Row reducing $[\mathbf{v}_1 \, \mathbf{v}_2 \, \mathbf{v}_3]$ shows the matrix does not have a pivot in each row. Therefore, by Theorem 4, the columns do not span $\mathbb{R}^4$.
