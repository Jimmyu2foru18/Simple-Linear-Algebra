Section 1.6: Applications of Linear Systems

What is this section about?

What are applications of linear systems? Linear systems can model real-world phenomena such as economic equilibrium, where input-output analysis determines equilibrium prices across sectors of an economy.

Core Lecture, Gilbert Strang (MIT)

Concept Overview

Linear systems can model economic equilibrium using input-output analysis. Each column of an exchange table describes where a sector's output goes, and the entries in each column sum to 1.

Equilibrium prices satisfy: income = expenses for each sector.

Worked Problems from linearAlgebraSolutionsComplete.pdf

Problem 1

Problem: Two-sector economy (Goods and Services). Find equilibrium prices.

Solution

Exchange table: Goods sector: 0.2 to Goods, 0.7 to Services. Services sector: 0.8 to Goods, 0.3 to Services.

Equilibrium equations:

$$0.8p_G + 0.7p_S = p_G \quad \Rightarrow \quad 0.7p_S = 0.2p_G \quad \Rightarrow \quad p_G = 0.875p_S$$

The general solution is $p_G = 0.875p_S$ with $p_S$ free. One solution: $p_S = 1000$, $p_G = 875$.

Only the ratio of prices matters: $p_G = \frac{7}{8}p_S$.

Problem 3

Problem: Three-sector economy (Chemicals, Fuels, Machinery). Find equilibrium prices.

Solution

Exchange table and equilibrium equations lead to the system:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 0.8 & -0.8 & -0.4 \\ -0.3 & 0.9 & -0.4 \\ -0.5 & -0.1 & 0.8 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} p_C \\ p_F \\ p_M \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

Row reducing gives the general solution:

$p_C = 1.417p_M$, $p_F = 0.917p_M$, with $p_M$ free.

If $p_M = 100$, then $p_C = 141.7$ and $p_F = 91.7$. Only the ratios matter.
