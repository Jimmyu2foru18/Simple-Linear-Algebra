What is this section about?

What is a linear transformation? A linear transformation T: R⁻¹ → R⁻ is a function that preserves vector addition and scalar multiplication: T(u + v) = T(u) + T(v) and T(cu) = cT(u).

Core Lecture, Gilbert Strang (MIT)

Concept Overview

A transformation $T$ from $\mathbb{R}^n$ to $\mathbb{R}^m$ is a rule that assigns to each vector $\mathbf{x}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ a vector $T(\mathbf{x})$ in $\mathbb{R}^m$.

$T$ is a linear transformation if:

  • $T(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = T(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v})$ for all $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$
  • $T(c\mathbf{u}) = cT(\mathbf{u})$ for all $\mathbf{u}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ and scalars $c$

Every linear transformation $T: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$ is a matrix transformation $T(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$ for some unique $m \times n$ matrix $A$, called the standard matrix of $T$.

Worked Problems from linearAlgebraSolutionsComplete.pdf

Problem 1

Problem: Find $T(\mathbf{u})$ and $T(\mathbf{v})$ where $T(\mathbf{x}) = A\mathbf{x}$, $A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{u} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}$, $\mathbf{v} = \begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \end{bmatrix}$.

Solution

$$T(\mathbf{u}) = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix}$$

$$T(\mathbf{v}) = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 1 & 3 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} a \\ b \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2a \\ a + 3b \end{bmatrix}$$

Problem 3

Problem: Determine whether $A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ has a solution, and if so, whether it is unique.

Solution

Row reducing the augmented matrix $[A \, | \, \mathbf{b}]$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 & | & 1 \\ 2 & 1 & 6 & | & 7 \\ 3 & 2 & 5 & | & 3 \end{bmatrix} \sim \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 & | & 1 \\ 0 & 1 & 2 & | & 5 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & | & 2 \end{bmatrix}$$

Every column of the coefficient matrix is a pivot column, so there are no free variables. The solution is unique.

$A\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}$ has a unique solution.

Problem 14

Problem: Geometric description of the linear transformation.

Solution

The transformation is a contraction by a factor of 0.5 (scaling all vectors by 1/2 toward the origin).

Problem 19

Problem: Find $T(\mathbf{x})$ where $T$ is linear, $T(\mathbf{e}_1) = \mathbf{y}_1$, $T(\mathbf{e}_2) = \mathbf{y}_2$, and $\mathbf{x} = 5\mathbf{e}_1 - 3\mathbf{e}_2$.

Solution

By linearity:

$$T(\mathbf{x}) = T(5\mathbf{e}_1 - 3\mathbf{e}_2) = 5T(\mathbf{e}_1) - 3T(\mathbf{e}_2) = 5\mathbf{y}_1 - 3\mathbf{y}_2$$

$$T(\mathbf{x}) = 5\begin{bmatrix} y_{11} \\ y_{21} \end{bmatrix} - 3\begin{bmatrix} y_{12} \\ y_{22} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 5y_{11} - 3y_{12} \\ 5y_{21} - 3y_{22} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 21 \\ 3 \\ 5 \\ 6 \\ 7 \end{bmatrix}$$

Problem 23

Problem: Geometric problem illustrating properties of linear transformations (scaling, sums, scalar multiples).

Solution

The figure shows that $T(c\mathbf{u}) = cT(\mathbf{u})$ and $T(\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}) = T(\mathbf{u}) + T(\mathbf{v})$ geometrically — scalar multiples and sums of vectors map to corresponding scalar multiples and sums of their images.

Problem 27

Problem: Show that a linear transformation maps line segments to line segments (or points).

Solution

For $\mathbf{x} = (1-t)\mathbf{p} + t\mathbf{q}$ with $0 \leq t \leq 1$:

$$T(\mathbf{x}) = T((1-t)\mathbf{p} + t\mathbf{q}) = (1-t)T(\mathbf{p}) + tT(\mathbf{q})$$

If $T(\mathbf{p})$ and $T(\mathbf{q})$ are distinct, this is the line segment between $T(\mathbf{p})$ and $T(\mathbf{q})$. If $T(\mathbf{p}) = T(\mathbf{q})$, the image is the single point $T(\mathbf{p})$.

Problem 29

Problem: Is $f(x) = mx + b$ a linear transformation from $\mathbb{R}$ to $\mathbb{R}$?

Solution

When $b = 0$: $f(x) = mx$ is linear because $f(cx + dy) = m(cx + dy) = cmx + dmy = c f(x) + d f(y)$.

When $b \neq 0$: $f(0) = m(0) + b = b \neq 0$. Every linear transformation maps $\mathbf{0}$ to $\mathbf{0}$, so $f$ is not linear.

Problem 37

Problem: [MATLAB problem] Row reduce a $4 \times 5$ matrix and find the general solution.

Solution

Row reducing the augmented matrix gives:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 & | & 14/4 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & | & 24/4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & | & 3 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & | & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

$x_4$ is free, $x_1 = 14/4 = 7/2$, $x_2 = 24/4 = 6$, $x_3 = 0$.

$$\mathbf{x} = x_4 \begin{bmatrix} 7/2 \\ 9/2 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}$$
